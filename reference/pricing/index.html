It's a spatial workspace. Instead of filing things into folders, you put your notes, files, images and links onto an infinite canvas and arrange them in a way that matches how you think conceptually. The layout is the organisation - similar ideas sit near each other, and connections represent relationships. It preserves causal relationships between ideas this way, so your brain can focus on what it does best: generating new ones.

Causal is the step before opening any creative tool. It's for creatives who see the benefit in planning before execution. Notion organises structure into pages and databases; Obsidian is plain markdown files. Causal's canvas stores rich text, files and images without restraining you to the structure of a page, so you have complete freedom in planning.

Yes, and it isn't a trial - the free plan doesn't expire and you don't need a card to start. You get 100 notes, images and links, 10 files, 5 widgets, 50 AI requests a month, and every feature the canvas has: nothing is locked behind Pro. Pro raises those limits for when you outgrow them.

Yes. Any canvas can be turned into a read-only link in one click, and whoever you send it to can open it without an account. Sharing grants access to that canvas and any nested canvases within it. You can copy the link again or stop sharing at any point.

Three tools provide structure. Create a collection to pack nodes into tidy columns. Create a nested canvas inside a canvas when a topic needs its own space. And when you have something you don't want to place yet, drag it to the right edge and park it in the unsorted tray.

You can drop in anything up to 20 MB per file. PDFs, Word documents, Adobe files, markdown, spreadsheets, images and video all open fullscreen inside the app with real previews. Markdown files and CSVs can even be edited in place, saving straight back to the file. Anything else is stored safely and shows up as a card you can download whenever you need it.
